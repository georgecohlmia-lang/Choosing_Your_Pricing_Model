STEP ONE · YOUR BUSINESS

A few details about what you're pricing.

The worksheet works for any offer with a single dominant customer pathway. If you have two genuinely distinct pathways (e.g., direct buyers and intermediary partners), you'll want to run the worksheet twice — once for each.

THE PRINCIPLE THAT GOVERNS THIS WORKSHEET
Structure first. Number after.
Most founders do this backwards: weeks on what to charge, zero time on how to charge. The price is built on top of the model. Get the model wrong and no number fixes the structural problem underneath. This worksheet is calibrated to keep you working on the model first.

Customer first, product second. The customer's behavior drives the model.

e.g., "Skincare products for cancer patients in active treatment." / "Bookkeeping services for service-based startups under $5M revenue." / "A productivity tool for product managers at mid-size SaaS companies."

Honest answer. The worksheet checks your assumption against your customer's actual behavior. If they match, that's a confirmation. If they don't, the diagnosis wins.

STEP TWO · THE THREE DIAGNOSTIC QUESTIONS

The three questions that decide the model.

The order matters. The first question determines whether subscription is even on the table. The second determines whether you're selling directly or through an intermediary. The third is the reality check.

How often does your customer actually need this? Fight the urge to force recurring revenue onto a fundamentally one-time purchase. Customers feel that.

The user and the buyer aren't always the same person. The trusted influencer between you and the buyer changes the model entirely.

Please answer both questions before continuing.

STEP THREE · YOUR ACTUAL CAPACITY

Question 3 — what can you sustain right now?

Match the model to your real capacity today, not what you'll have in twelve months. Models that need infrastructure you don't yet have will collapse before they generate revenue.

Honest assessment. \"We could build it\" doesn't count. \"It's running today\" or \"it's set up and ready to run within 30 days\" counts.
